Appendix F — What C23 added, and where this book covers it

Every edition of the standard records “what changed” about itself (C23’s annex M.2). Taking that list as the frame, this appendix gathers where in this book each item is covered. It is a place for someone who already knows the new standard to ask “which chapter tells that story”, and equally for someone who has finished the book to check “did I meet all of C23”.

★ This book does not teach C23 separately. Rather than teaching the old way first and appending the new, it explains in C23 from the start and adds “it used to be like this” only where that helps. So the items below do not sit together; they are scattered through their own subjects. The three tables below stitch those places back together.

How to use these tables#

  • To skim only what is new — follow the “Where” column of the three tables above and you have gone once around C23.
  • To check your compiler keeps up — read chapter 19’s edition table together with __STDC_VERSION__ and the per-header version macros. The language mode (-std=c23) is opened by the compiler, while headers such as <stdbit.h> come from the platform’s C library, so check the two separately (a header with __has_include(<stdbit.h>)). ★ Being in the standard is not being in the compiler — chapter 97 measures two compilers ignoring [[unsequenced]].
  • To move old code over — the last section of chapter 97 sets out an order.
